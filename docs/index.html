Repository and installation

Understand every recommendation.

What Energy Compass entities mean, how their values are calculated, and which assumptions sit behind the plan.

This guide describes the Home Assistant integration in v0.1.28. Examples use PLN; your installation uses its configured currency. Names are shown in English and Polish.

Reading the compass

Energy Compass answers two different questions. Consumption compass estimates whether additional household use is attractive during the current display interval. Energy compass summarizes what the optimized battery, solar and grid plan proposes for the first native interval.

Advisory only. The integration does not charge the battery, change an inverter limit, curtail panels or execute notification actions. Results assume that the proposed plan can be followed. Existing inverter behavior may produce different costs.

  1. Check validityForecast valid must be on. Check current guidance validity before using a consumption recommendation.
  2. Read the cost and levelThe cost describes an extra energy probe over a time interval, not the electricity tariff at this instant.
  3. Inspect the planReview flows, future windows, source coverage and equipment assumptions.

kW measures power; kWh measures energy. An 8 kW flow lasting 15 minutes transfers 2 kWh. Plan flows are interval energies in kWh, not live power readings.

On narrow screens, scroll tables horizontally to read all columns. Keyboard users can focus a table region and use the arrow keys.

Inputs and assumptions

What feeds the calculation
InputMeaning and treatment
Import and export pricesChoose each side in Tariffs as a fixed rate, a numeric sensor/number/input_number state or attribute held constant across the known horizon, or an interval forecast. Currency/MWh is divided by 1,000. Buy and sell transformations are independent; no currency conversion occurs.
Solar forecastInterval energy, or average power multiplied by elapsed hours. For example, 4 kW over 30 minutes becomes 2 kWh. Today/tomorrow continuations merge; only explicitly separate arrays add together. A daily total alone cannot place production in time. Disabled PV contributes zero.

Battery SOCCurrent stored energy, derived from a selected percentage, fraction or kWh source. Capacity, freshness, plausibility and optional independent BMS agreement are checked. Battery-disabled installations do not require SOC.
Battery constraintsCapacity, operating floor, ceiling, charge/discharge power, separate efficiencies and wear price. The operating floor must be at least the hardware floor. The solver uses the operating floor as its reserve.
Site constraintsInverter power, grid import and export limits, and explicit permission to model grid charging, battery export and curtailment. These are planning capabilities, not device commands.
Horizons and timingPlanning horizon, display horizon, percentile reference horizon, display interval, source freshness and compute budgets. Missing future coverage can shorten the usable plan.

Tariff transformation

effective price = normalized source price × multiplier × VAT factor + addition

VAT factor = 1 + VAT percent / 100 when enabled; otherwise 1

The addition is applied after the VAT factor. Fixed rates use the same transformation. Include applicable variable charges once; do not apply VAT again to a source that already contains it. Fixed monthly charges are reporting metadata and do not affect dispatch or the extra-kWh calculation.

Negative prices remain valid in the generic integration. Clipping an export price to zero or applying a deposit valuation factor requires an explicitly prepared source or suitable configuration; neither is an automatic assumption for every RCE installation.

First setup requires separate confirmation of the displayed buy source and household load source. Every preview and save validates current inputs and solves a bounded base plan. An infeasible or timed-out plan cannot be saved; extra-consumption guidance is calculated after saving. Existing installations retain their original final confirmation.

Load forecast from history

The Plan and Forecast valid sensors expose load_quality: selected source, actual forecast method, per-segment history sample counts, and fallback coverage. fallback_fraction measures elapsed forecast time, including separate repeated daylight-saving hours. Recorder fallback raises load_history_fallback even when its estimated energy is zero. This detailed attribute is excluded from recorder history.

The history model averages complete hourly consumption samples by local hour and day group. With group_weekends enabled, weekdays share one group and weekends another; otherwise each weekday has its own group. Defaults use 14 days of history and at least two samples per bucket.

Each forecast slot receives its elapsed share of the matching hourly mean. Recorder statistics use reset-adjusted cumulative sums; power history is integrated over complete hours with coverage checks. Missing buckets use fallback_daily_kwh / 24 only when fallback is enabled. An explicit fixed daily estimate is also distributed uniformly at daily_load_kwh / 24 kWh per elapsed hour.

Timestamp identity and durations use UTC; day grouping uses the configured local timezone. Repeated daylight-saving hours remain distinct intervals.

Generic integration defaults and configurable limits

These are setup defaults in settings.py, not a statement about your saved options. The standalone engine helper has different default absolute thresholds; Home Assistant passes the configured values explicitly.

  • Planning, display and reference horizons: 24 hours each; configurable from 1 to 48 hours. Display and reference horizons cannot exceed the requested planning horizon.
  • Display interval: 60 minutes; options are 15, 30 or 60 minutes. At most 96 distinct probes are allowed across display and reference horizons.
  • Extra-energy probe: 1 kWh; UI range 0.01–5 kWh.
  • Flexible-load profiles: 3, 5, 10, 15 and 20 kWh; default power cap 3 kW and allowed block-price degradation 15%.
  • BOOST ceiling: 0.01 currency/kWh. LIMIT floor: 1 currency/kWh. CHEAP/LIMIT percentiles: 25th/75th.
  • Probe-failure policy: absolute_fallback. Grid charging, battery export and curtailment: disabled.
  • Default budgets: 10 seconds for the base plan, 2 seconds per probe and 60 seconds overall. Performance allows up to 30 seconds for each solve and 300 seconds overall. Probes share the remaining time after the base calculation and a one-second finishing reserve.
  • Default periodic refresh: 15 minutes, configurable up to one hour. Significant source changes can trigger earlier recalculation; validity boundaries only validate sources and advance cached advice.

How calculation works

1. Build a baseline plan

The integration aligns source intervals without averaging away tariff boundaries, then solves a mixed-integer optimization using SciPy HiGHS. Each slot chooses import, export, battery charge, battery discharge, curtailed solar and end-of-slot battery energy.

PV − curtailment + import + discharge = load + export + charge

next stored energy = previous stored energy + ηcharge × charge − discharge / ηdischarge

objective J = Σ(buy × import − sell × export + wear × (charge + discharge) / 2) − terminal credit

All flow terms are kWh per native slot. Prices and wear use currency/kWh. Charge and discharge efficiencies, ηcharge and ηdischarge, are fractions between 0 and 1.

  • Grid import and export cannot happen simultaneously within a slot; neither can battery charge and discharge.
  • Power limits become slot-energy limits by multiplying kW by elapsed hours. The inverter bounds the magnitude of PV − curtailment + discharge − charge; grid limits apply separately.
  • With grid charging disabled, charging cannot exceed forecast solar surplus. With battery export disabled, discharge cannot exceed the forecast household deficit after PV.
  • With curtailment disabled, its value is fixed at zero. Incompatible generation, load, battery and export constraints may then make a plan infeasible.
  • The default preserve_initial terminal rule requires final battery energy at least equal to initial energy. Its terminal credit is zero. In value mode, final energy is credited at terminal_value_per_kwh instead.
  • An enabled daily cycle cap limits AC-side charge-plus-discharge throughput to 2 × capacity × daily_cycles per local date, less today's observed throughput. SOC efficiency is modeled separately; DC counters need upstream conversion. Zero disables the cap; a positive cap requires a current observed-throughput source.

The solver normally proves an optimum within its limit; if the limit runs out first, the best validated solution found so far is published with time_limited and its mip_gap. Returned flows are independently checked with a 0.000001 kWh absolute tolerance. These are AC-equivalent energy flows; equipment efficiency and usable capacity still need validation against measurements.

2. Add hypothetical consumption

For every display/reference interval, the integration adds a probe—normally 1 kWh—to forecast household demand. The added energy is spread uniformly in time across that interval and allocated to native slots by elapsed overlap.

extra load in slot = probe kWh × overlapping seconds / display-interval seconds

extra-kWh cost = (J with extra load − baseline J) / actual added kWh

It then reoptimizes the whole available planning horizon with prices, starting SOC, terminal rules and equipment limits unchanged. Extra consumption can cause more import, less export, different battery use or different future charging. The resulting cost includes those opportunity costs and any change in wear or terminal credit.

The interval starts at the snapshot time. With a 60-minute display interval, a snapshot at 21:35 covers 21:35–22:35. It can cross a tariff change at 22:00. It is not a price for consuming the entire extra kWh immediately.

A 1 kWh probe over 60 minutes adds an average 1 kW; over 15 minutes it adds 4 kW. Different probe sizes can produce different per-kWh costs when constraints bind. The last interval overlapping source coverage is clipped and probed at its actual duration without scaling down the configured energy. A very short tail therefore requires higher average power and can fail when equipment limits bind. That tail remains unknown while successful full reference intervals retain percentile guidance. Later intervals, infeasible probes and expired probe budgets remain unknown; they are not assigned a zero cost.

3. Compare with the reference horizon

Successful reference costs are sorted and configured percentiles are calculated by linear interpolation: for percentile p, position (n − 1) × p / 100 lies between two sorted costs. A failed source-clipped tail is excluded when at least one reference probe succeeded; a failed full interval invokes the configured policy. The minimum purchase tariff is taken independently from source slots overlapping the available reference range. Classification uses the rules in Consumption compass.

Reference coverage concerns the percentile horizon, not necessarily the entire planning horizon. If contiguous source coverage is shorter than the configured reference horizon, percentiles use that available coverage and the result reports it explicitly. Missing future prices do not by themselves invoke the probe-failure policy.

Consumption compass

consumption_compass · Kompas zużycia · Sensor

The level of the first consumption outlook interval. Let c be its extra-kWh cost, B the BOOST ceiling, L the LIMIT floor, Qlow/Qhigh the configured reference percentiles, and Pmin the minimum purchase tariff in the available reference horizon. Rules are evaluated in the order shown; the first match wins.

StateExact rule with complete referenceMeaning
BOOSTc < BExtra use costs less than your configured BOOST ceiling. The default ceiling is 0.01 currency/kWh; real negative costs remain BOOST.
CHEAPc ≤ Qlow or c ≤ PminExtra use is no more expensive than the low percentile or the minimum purchase tariff in the available reference horizon.
LIMITc > L and c > QhighExtra use exceeds both your absolute expensive threshold and the upper reference percentile.
NORMALAll other known costsNone of the other rules matched. This is not a statement that the battery is full or the instantaneous tariff is low.

BOOST wins first, followed by CHEAP, LIMIT and NORMAL. Equality at the low percentile or minimum purchase tariff is CHEAP; a negligible numeric tolerance covers solver residue at those equalities. Equality at the BOOST ceiling is not BOOST, and equality at the LIMIT floor or high percentile does not trigger LIMIT.

With absolute_fallback, a failed full reference probe removes percentile tests but retains the independent minimum-tariff CHEAP rule. BOOST uses c < B; CHEAP uses c ≤ Pmin; LIMIT uses c > L; everything else known is NORMAL. With the unavailable policy, a full-interval failure suppresses levels. A failed source-clipped tail remains unknown without suppressing percentile advice from successful reference probes.

Unavailable: the baseline is invalid/expired, or the current guidance level is unknown. A low SOC is incorporated into the optimization; it does not independently force LIMIT.

Consumption cost

consumption_cost · Koszt zużycia · Currency/kWh

The finite-difference cost of the current display interval: (probe objective − baseline objective) / added kWh. Positive means extra modeled cost; zero means no change; negative means the extra load reduces the modeled objective. This is neither an electricity-meter reading nor the current buy rate.

Attributes include method: finite_difference and probe_kwh. Display rounding uses cost_precision; classification uses the unrounded value.

Unavailable: same gate as Consumption compass. Under the strict unavailable policy, this sensor is unavailable even if a numeric probe result survives in plan.outlook but its level is unknown.

Flexible energy depth

flexible_energy_depth · Głębokość elastycznego zużycia · kWh

Five independent counterfactual plans add 3, 5, 10, 15 and 20 kWh of flexible demand to the same baseline. Each profile can schedule at up to the configured power cap, 3 kW by default. The optimizer includes tariffs, PV, battery losses and wear, terminal value, grid limits and operating constraints.

anchor price = (objective3 − baseline objective) / 3

allowed block price = anchor price + 15% × |anchor price|

block pricea→b = (incremental costb − incremental costa) / (b − a)

The published state is the largest consecutive profile whose next block stays at or below the allowed price. The first degraded or unknown block stops depth; a later stable block cannot skip that gap. Negative anchors use the same absolute-difference rule, so −0.10 permits values through −0.085. A zero anchor permits no unfavorable positive increase.

Attributes expose the 3 kWh anchor price, allowed block price, configured power and degradation, currency, and all profile summaries. Profiles are grouped as small (3/5 kWh), medium (10/15 kWh) and large (20 kWh). Each includes total incremental cost, average and marginal block prices, import/export and battery-throughput deltas, status, failure reason and nonzero schedule rows.

This sensor measures price depth, not whether electricity is cheap in absolute terms. A stable depth can still carry an expensive anchor price. Compare anchor_price_per_kwh with the tariff or household policy before starting a load.

Unavailable: the baseline is invalid/expired, flexible-depth analysis is disabled, or the 3 kWh anchor cannot be solved within coverage, power and time limits.

Next change

next_change · Następna zmiana · Timestamp

The start of the first later outlook interval whose known consumption level differs from the first interval. Attribute next_level gives that level. It tracks consumption guidance, not an energy-state change or every tariff change.

The scan stops at the first later unknown level. If the current level itself is unknown, the entity implementation can return the immediately following known level; interpret this as the next known guidance, not a confirmed transition from a known current state.

Unavailable: no qualifying transition appears before the scan stops, or the baseline is invalid/expired.

Next boost start

next_boost_start · Początek zwiększonego zużycia · Timestamp

The start of the first active or upcoming contiguous BOOST window. Adjacent BOOST intervals merge. An active window can have a start timestamp in the past; it is not skipped in favor of tomorrow's window.

Unavailable: no BOOST window exists in the calculated outlook, or the baseline is invalid/expired. This does not assert that BOOST can never occur beyond forecast coverage.

Next cheap start

next_cheap_start · Początek taniego okresu · Timestamp

The start of the first active or upcoming contiguous CHEAP window. BOOST windows are separate; this entity does not mean “any favorable period.” The plan's favorable_windows combines adjacent BOOST and CHEAP intervals.

Unavailable: no CHEAP window exists, the baseline is invalid/expired, or the classification mode cannot produce CHEAP.

Next limit start

next_limit_start · Początek ograniczenia · Timestamp

The start of the first active or upcoming contiguous LIMIT window. It indicates an expensive additional-consumption period; it does not restrict inverter power or shut off household loads.

Unavailable: no LIMIT window exists in coverage, or the baseline is invalid/expired.

Attributes shared by the three window-start sensors

status
active when now falls inside the window; otherwise upcoming.
end, duration_minutes, remaining_minutes
Window end, total duration and time remaining. Before an upcoming window starts, remaining minutes equals its full duration.
start_basis
forecast for a predicted start; first_observed for an active window anchored when first observed. Overlapping active starts are preserved across recalculation and restart while the stored anchor remains applicable.

Home Assistant can omit dynamic attributes from unavailable entities. For absent windows, inspect plan.window_status: none_in_coverage, active or upcoming. Known later windows can remain available after a failed current probe.

Energy compass

energy_compass · Kompas energii · Sensor

One primary label for the first native plan interval. This interval can be shorter than the consumption display interval. Rules use interval kWh and a tolerance ε = 0.000001 kWh, in this exact precedence:

StateRuleInterpretation
CURTAILcurtailment > εSome available solar production is left unused in the plan. Takes precedence over any simultaneous battery flow.
CHARGE_GRIDcharge > max(PV − load, 0) + εCharging exceeds available forecast solar surplus, indicating grid-fed charging. Some solar can also contribute.
CHARGE_PVcharge > ε, after the rules aboveBattery charging fits within forecast solar surplus.
DISCHARGE_GRIDdischarge > ε and export > εBattery discharge coincides with aggregate grid export. Aggregate flows do not uniquely prove which source supplied each exported kWh.
SELF_CONSUMEdischarge > ε, without the conditions aboveBattery discharge serves household demand without material grid export.
HOLDNo rule above matchedNo material battery charge/discharge or curtailment. PV can still power the home or export; the home can still import.

Pure solar self-consumption with an idle battery is HOLD, not SELF_CONSUME. Labels summarize proposed aggregate flows and do not confirm inverter mode, command execution or measured power.

Attributes contain the first interval's start, end, buy_per_kwh, sell_per_kwh, pv_kwh, load_kwh, grid_import_kwh, grid_export_kwh, charge_kwh, discharge_kwh, curtail_kwh and end_soc_kwh. The last value is stored energy, not a percentage.

Unavailable: the baseline is invalid/expired. A failed consumption probe alone does not hide a valid energy plan.

Expected net cost

expected_net_cost · Przewidywany koszt netto · Currency

Σ(import kWh × buy price − export kWh × sell price)

Calculated from the baseline over the display horizon, shortened to available coverage. A slot crossing the display end is counted proportionally. Attribute coverage_hours gives the actual duration.

Positive means modeled net spending; negative means export value exceeds import cost. “Net” means imports minus exports, not “excluding VAT.” Export value may represent a settlement credit rather than cash paid out.

This value excludes battery wear, terminal credit and fixed monthly charges. It is not automatically equal to the full optimizer objective or the final electricity bill.

Unavailable: the baseline is invalid/expired.

Expected wear cost

expected_wear_cost · Przewidywany koszt zużycia baterii · Currency

Σ(wear price × (charge kWh + discharge kWh) / 2)

Uses the same display coverage and proportional boundary treatment as Expected net cost, with coverage_hours. Wear is a configured economic assumption, not a battery-health measurement or supplier fee.

At 0.05 PLN/kWh, charging 2 kWh and discharging 1.9 kWh costs 0.05 × (2 + 1.9) / 2 = 0.0975 PLN. A battery-free plan has zero wear.

Unavailable: the baseline is invalid/expired.

Alert

alert · Alert · Diagnostic problem binary sensor

on: an input check or calculation failed. code identifies SOC jumps, invalid input, timeout, infeasibility or calculation errors; reason provides detail. since marks the first occurrence of the current problem, plan_retained identifies a covered previous plan, and last_successful_plan_at identifies its generation. A retry keeps the alert; successful publication clears it.

A corrected SOC can establish a new reference after fresh reports remain physically plausible for at least 60 seconds. Both the first and preceding recovery reports are checked. Missing/invalid readings or a new jump interrupt stabilization. Repeating the same timestamp is insufficient. The existing plan continues during this process.

Forecast valid

forecast_valid · Poprawna prognoza · Binary sensor

on: a valid baseline exists and current UTC time is strictly before valid_until. off: no published plan covers the current time. This entity remains available to report that condition.

Normally valid_until is bounded by periodic recalculation, forecast coverage and source freshness. During a refresh or failure, plan_retained=true keeps the last plan available until its actual coverage end; Alert reports input and calculation problems separately. Native interval boundaries advance current advice and commitment clocks without a new optimization. Fresh reports of unchanged inputs can extend freshness within those bounds; significant changes still request an earlier solve. An unchanged numeric SOC still needs a trustworthy report timestamp; availability is not established by its percentage alone. The default auto policy uses Home Assistant’s native last_reported for native SOC and BMS timestamp paths, including older saved last_updated paths. An identical fresh report therefore stays fresh. Custom timestamp paths retain their exact meaning; exact_path also preserves literal native-field selection.

current_guidance_valid
Whether the current consumption outlook has a known level. Use it together with the binary sensor's on state; it is generation metadata and is not itself a clock-based freshness check.
coverage_end, requested_end, coverage_complete
Actual source horizon, requested planning end, and whether that entire requested horizon is covered. These are distinct from reference-horizon classification.
input_ages
Snapshot ages in seconds based on Home Assistant last_updated. Source-specific publication and SOC report timestamps can impose additional freshness checks.
missing_sources
Missing continuation identifiers or the invalidation reason, depending on whether a valid baseline exists.

Forecast valid does not certify tariff accuracy, battery capacity, perfect forecasts or physical execution. Those assumptions need separate validation.

Validity and missing data

Entities share generated_at, valid_until, reason, reasons, calibration, capacity_calibration and classification_mode attributes when available. reason describes invalidation or reference coverage; reasons combines quality warnings and any incomplete-reference reason.

Plan continuity. A queued or failed refresh keeps the previous covered plan operating with plan_retained=true. Native intervals and windows advance with time. refreshing=true means a replacement is queued or running. Input errors and failed calculations turn Alert on; they do not erase the plan. Successful publication replaces it and clears the alert. Coverage never extends beyond the last forecast interval. Unknown probes and ended windows remain unavailable. Restart or integration reload requires a new plan.

absolute_fallbackA full reference probe failed, or no reference probe succeeded. Known costs use absolute BOOST/LIMIT and minimum-tariff CHEAP rules.
unavailableA full reference probe failed, or no reference probe succeeded, and the selected strict policy suppresses consumption levels. The baseline can remain valid.

Calibration attributes are unvalidated or verified. They reflect configured validation status, not an automatic measurement or certification. Detailed input/solver failures can appear as other reason strings.

Unavailable is not zero, HOLD or NORMAL. Do not replace missing recommendations with a favorable default. An absent window may simply mean no matching period is known within current coverage.

For consumption-driven automations, check Forecast valid is on and current_guidance_valid is true, then compare stable uppercase levels. Optional window notifications also require Notify enabled and a configured action in the notification blueprint.

These are explanatory scenarios, not live readings, recommended tariff settings or guaranteed savings. Their values illustrate the formulas above.

Battery capacity is not available energy

Example assumptions

Initial stored energy = 25 × 0.11 = 2.75 kWh

Minimum stored energy = 25 × 0.10 = 2.50 kWh

Stored energy above reserve = 2.75 − 2.50 = 0.25 kWh

Deliverable energy above reserve ≈ 0.25 × 0.974679 = 0.24367 kWh

At 11% SOC, this 25 kWh battery has only about 0.244 kWh available to supply AC loads before the planning reserve, subject to power and other constraints. The optimizer cannot treat the nominal 25 kWh as immediately available.

Why NORMAL can coexist with a 1.25 PLN/kWh tariff

Use a deliberately simple battery-disabled, PV-disabled case to isolate the display-interval effect. Load is a fixed 24 kWh/day (1 kW), the grid import limit is 25 kW, export is zero, and no equipment limit binds. The snapshot is at 21:30; planning, display and reference horizons are 24 hours; probes are 1 kWh across 60-minute intervals.

Assume import costs 1.25 PLN/kWh from 06:00–22:00 and 0.61 PLN/kWh from 22:00–06:00, repeating daily. Export price and wear are zero; there is no battery terminal credit. BOOST ceiling is 0.01 and LIMIT floor is 1 PLN/kWh; reference percentiles are 25th/75th and all probes succeed.

Extra demand at 21:30–22:00: 0.5 kWh × 1.25 = 0.625 PLN

Extra demand at 22:00–22:30: 0.5 kWh × 0.61 = 0.305 PLN

Consumption cost = (0.625 + 0.305) / 1 kWh = 0.930 PLN/kWh

The 24 reference costs contain seven full low-rate hours at 0.61, two mixed hours at 0.93, and fifteen full high-rate hours at 1.25. Thus Q25 = 0.61 and Q75 = 1.25. The current 0.93 is above BOOST, below the LIMIT floor, and not below Q25: NORMAL.

Baseline import cost is 8 × 0.61 + 16 × 1.25 = 24.88 PLN. The probe increases the objective to 25.81 PLN; the difference is 0.93 PLN. Energy compass is HOLD, Expected wear cost is 0, and Forecast valid is on while this generation remains fresh.

The first fully low-rate display interval begins at 22:30 in this snapshot's outlook, not at the 22:00 tariff boundary, because display bins are anchored to 21:30. Low-rate bins equal both Q25 and the minimum purchase tariff, so they are CHEAP. High-rate bins equal Q75 and remain NORMAL. The mixed current interval remains NORMAL, with the next CHEAP window beginning at 22:30.

Adding a nearly empty battery changes feasible flows and costs, but introduces no independent “low SOC means LIMIT” rule.

Solar consumption can have an export opportunity cost

In a separate one-hour example, disable the battery and assume 2 kWh PV, 1 kWh household demand, a 0.40 PLN/kWh export value, a 1.25 PLN/kWh import rate and nonbinding inverter/grid limits. The baseline exports 1 kWh, so its net objective is −0.40 PLN.

A 1 kWh extra-load probe consumes that surplus. Import remains zero, export falls to zero and the objective becomes 0 PLN:

Extra-kWh cost = (0 − (−0.40)) / 1 = 0.40 PLN/kWh

No extra grid purchase occurred, but export value was lost. With a battery, the same comparison can also include changed charging, discharging, future purchases and wear. The integration recomputes the full plan instead of assuming every extra kWh costs the current buy rate.

Curtailment

Curtailment means deliberately leaving some available solar production unused. It differs from battery charging, exporting surplus or reducing household consumption.

Example: during one hour, PV could produce 8 kWh, the home needs 1 kWh, the battery is full and export is disallowed. A feasible plan with curtailment can use 1 kWh and curtail 7 kWh. Curtailment may also become economical with negative export prices, depending on the rest of the plan.

  1. Open Settings → Devices & services → Energy Compass → Configure.
  2. Open Hardware capabilities (Możliwości sprzętu).
  3. Enable Allow curtailment (Ograniczanie produkcji PV), then use Preview and save (Podgląd i zapis).

This allows curtail_kwh to be positive in the optimizer; it does not force curtailment or operate the inverter. Enabling a capability that equipment cannot follow makes the advisory plan unrealistic.

Physical curtailment needs a separate control layer. That layer needs compatible inverter generation/export controls, verified metering and operating-mode behavior, and a way to restore normal limits. Energy Compass v0.1.2 provides no such controller.

An 8 kW PV system with an 8 kW permitted export path usually has room to export surplus, subject to shared inverter and other limits. When export value is explicitly floored at zero, negative raw market prices alone do not create a modeled export penalty. Allowing curtailment therefore does not guarantee a CURTAIL state.
